What is the problem?

The wisdom tooth is the last tooth at the back. Its other name is the third molar. An impacted lower wisdom tooth is stuck under the gum and bone. It has not come through the gum (it is unerupted). It cannot grow in the right way.

The nerve

A nerve runs in a tube of bone inside the lower jaw. Here it is called the jaw nerve. Its medical name is the inferior alveolar nerve. Two blood vessels run with it in the same tube. They are the inferior alveolar artery and vein.[[juodzbalys1]]

The jaw nerve gives feeling to the lower teeth and gums on the same side.[[sarikov]] Near the front of the jaw, a branch of it comes out of the bone through a small hole. This branch is the chin nerve (mental nerve). It gives feeling to the lower lip and chin.[[juodzbalys2]]

The tongue gets its feeling from a different nerve, the lingual nerve. It can also be hurt when a wisdom tooth is taken out.[[renton]]

In this model, the roots of the wisdom tooth wrap around the nerve and artery.

Why taking the tooth out can hurt the nerve

To take the tooth out, the roots must come out of the bone. The roots curve around the nerve. So the nerve can be pressed, stretched or cut as the roots come out. The artery and vein can also be hurt and bleed.[[juodzbalys1]]

If the jaw nerve is hurt, the chin nerve may also stop working well. This is because it is a branch of the jaw nerve.[[juodzbalys2]]

What nerve damage feels like

The lower lip, chin, teeth or gums on that side may:[[sarikov]]

  • feel numb
  • tingle, like pins and needles
  • feel different, or strange
  • hurt. Even a light touch may hurt.[[renton]]

The lip and face can still move as normal.[[sarikov]]

Short-term: the feeling comes back. This most often takes weeks to months.[[sarikov]]
Long-term: the feeling does not fully come back.[[sarikov]]

Age: the risk of nerve damage is higher in people over about 24 years old.[[sarikov]] In older people, the feeling is also less likely to come back fully.[[queral]] One small study followed people for 5 years. Everyone under 30 got full feeling back. Only 3 of 8 people over 30 did.[[kjolle]] Studies do not agree on one exact age.

How often does it happen?

Some wisdom teeth have roots close to the nerve. A review of 16 studies looked at these teeth. It found:[[rico]]

About 7 in 100had nerve damage after the whole tooth was taken out
About 1 in 100had nerve damage that did not go away

Why a 3D scan can help

The usual dental X-ray of the whole mouth is called an OPG. OPG is short for orthopantomogram. It is also called a panoramic X-ray. It is a flat picture. On a flat picture, the roots can look like they touch the nerve, even when they do not.[[rodriguez]] Some signs on an OPG can warn that the roots are close to the nerve.[[rood]]

A CBCT scan is a 3D X-ray. CBCT is short for cone beam computed tomography. It can show if the roots really touch the nerve. It also shows which side of the roots the nerve is on.[[rodriguez]] Studies show it does this better than an OPG.[[reia]]

A CBCT scan uses more X-ray than an OPG. So it is used when an OPG shows the roots may be close to the nerve.[[rodriguez]]

Choices your dentist/surgeon might offer

  • Leave the tooth in and check it often, if it is not causing problems.
  • Coronectomy: take out only the top of the tooth. Leave the roots around the nerve. In the same review, about 1 in 200 people had nerve damage after this.[[rico]] But the roots can move. Once in a while they need to come out later.
  • Take out the whole tooth. This is often a surgical extraction. A small cut is made in the gum. If bone is in the way of the tooth, some of it may be removed.

Before any treatment, the dentist or surgeon will explain the good points, risks and choices in much more detail.

Questions you could ask your dentist/surgeon

  • How close are the roots to the nerve?
  • What is the risk of nerve damage in this case?
  • Is a CBCT scan needed?
  • Is a coronectomy a good choice in this case?
  • What happens if the tooth is left in?
