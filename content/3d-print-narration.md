# 3D printed model and narrated video: 45-second narration script

This is the spoken script that plays while a participant looks at the 3D printed model. The narrated video uses the same script, with subtitles. No written card and no stickers go with the model, as in a clinic. The script points to parts by colour and position (the red nerve). Sources are the same as for the 2D information sheet and the AR web page. The risk figures rest on at least 3 reviews each: Rico-Barroso 2026, Derbishi 2026, Kang 2025 and Sarikov 2014 for about 7 in 100; Rico-Barroso 2026, Kang 2019 and Cervera-Espert 2016 for about 1 in 100; Kim 2012 and Cheung 2010 for recovery within months; Hillerup 2008, Gülicher 2001 and Leung 2019 for lasting damage being mostly mild (full list in content/references.json). Wording from the 29 October 2026 risk-figure review, applied 4 November 2026.

- **English:** 113 words, about 45 seconds at a comfortable pace (narrated video, natural-pace voice: see video/README.md). Flesch-Kincaid grade about 3.
- **Japanese:** option C (29 October 2026), 240 characters without readings. Natural adult kanji; kanji above grade 6 get furigana in the subtitles. Reviewed by three Claude models; still needs a native-speaker check.

To fit 45 seconds at a comfortable pace, the script leaves out these points from the sheet and web page: the tube in the bone, the artery and vein, the name of the chin nerve, the tongue nerve, the list of symptoms, the lasting-pain sentence, and the age effect. The recovery sentence from the roots paragraph moved into the risk paragraph on 4 November 2026 (recovery and severity are now said with the numbers). The last sentence follows referral guidance (Dental Board of Australia scope of practice 2020: refer when a case is beyond the dentist's competence; FDS RCS England 2020 parameters of care) and does not say a specialist lowers the risk, as no study shows that (see the "Specialist referral and reference check" review). "The nerve can be pressed or cut" follows suggested reference fix 3 from that review.

## English

This is an impacted lower wisdom tooth. Its roots wrap around a nerve. Taking it out can damage the nerve and make your lip and chin numb.

The red part is the nerve. It gives feeling to the lower teeth, gums, lip and chin.

As the tooth comes out, the nerve can be pressed or cut.

For a tooth like this, rates vary, but about 7 in 100 may get nerve damage. Most get better within a few months. About 1 in 100 may have damage that does not go away, and for most of them it is mild.

So it is a good idea to see a specialist (an oral surgeon) first.
