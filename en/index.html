Your lower wisdom tooth and the nerve beside it

What is the problem?

Your lower wisdom tooth is impacted. It is stuck under the gum and bone and cannot come through properly.

The nerve

Inside the lower jaw runs the inferior alveolar nerve, with an artery beside it. The nerve gives feeling to your lower lip, chin, lower teeth and gum on that side.

In this case the roots of the wisdom tooth wrap around the nerve and artery.

Why removing the tooth can injure the nerve

To remove the tooth, its roots must be lifted out of the bone. Because they curve around the nerve, the nerve can be bruised, stretched or cut as the roots come out. The artery beside it can also bleed.

What nerve injury feels like

Numbness, tingling or a changed feeling, sometimes pain, in the lower lip, chin, teeth and gum on that side. It does not stop the lip or face from moving.

Temporary: feeling comes back, usually over weeks to months.
Permanent: feeling does not fully come back.

How often does it happen?

For wisdom teeth whose roots are close to the nerve, a review of 16 studies found:

About 7 in 100had nerve injury after full removal
About 1 in 100had permanent injury

Source: Rico-Barroso L, et al. Med Oral Patol Oral Cir Bucal. 2026;31(1):e19-e30. doi.org/10.4317/medoral.27459

Options to discuss with your surgeon

  • Leave the tooth and check it regularly, if it is not causing problems.
  • Coronectomy: remove only the top of the tooth and leave the roots around the nerve. In the same review, nerve injury after coronectomy was about 1 in 200, but the roots sometimes move and occasionally need removing later.
  • Remove the whole tooth.

A 3D X-ray (CBCT scan) helps your surgeon see exactly where the nerve is.

Questions you can ask

  • How close are my roots to the nerve?
  • What is my own risk of nerve injury?
  • Is coronectomy an option for me?
  • What happens if I leave the tooth?
