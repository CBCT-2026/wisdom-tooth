Impacted mandibular first molar with root encircling the inferior alveolar nerve and an odontogenic cyst formation

Abstract

Background

Dentigerous cysts are among the most common benign lesions encountered in oral and maxillofacial surgery. While these cysts typically develop in association with impacted mandibular third molars, their occurrence in relation to mandibular first molars represents an exceptionally rare clinical entity. This study aims to systematically describe the surgical management, standardized postoperative care protocols, and long-term follow-up strategies for this rare presentation.

Case presentation

A 35-year-old female patient presented to our institution with a 12-month history of persistent buccal swelling in the left posterior mandibular region. Panoramic radiography demonstrated an impacted tooth #36 situated between teeth #35 and #37, surrounded by a well-defined unilocular radiolucent lesion measuring approximately 2.5 cm in diameter. Cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) further revealed a deeply impacted tooth #36 with complete circumferential encasement of the inferior alveolar canal by the tooth roots. Surgical management included en bloc cyst enucleation under local anesthesia, followed by minimally invasive extraction of tooth #36 using piezoelectric ultrasonic instrumentation. Postoperative pharmacological intervention comprised a 3-day methylprednisolone protocol (4 mg once daily) to control inflammation and mecobalamin supplementation (0.5 mg three times daily) to support neural recovery. One-year follow-up clinical and radiographic examinations confirmed complete resolution of the lesion with no evidence of recurrence or neurosensory deficits.

Conclusion

The impaction of mandibular first molars with concurrent odontogenic cysts represents a clinically rare entity, particularly when complicated by complete root encirclement of the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle. Following cyst enucleation, clinicians should consider orthodontic traction for functionally viable teeth or select surgical extraction when preservation is contraindicated. Preoperative CBCT evaluation remains mandatory to delineate three-dimensional root-neural topography. Staged surgical intervention—incorporating precision osteotomy, cyst decompression, and neurovascular liberation—effectively mitigates risks of neural injury and osseous defects.

Keywords: Dentigerous cyst, Mandibular nerve, Molar, Nerve injury, Tooth, Impacted

Background

Dentigerous cysts, one of the most common odontogenic cysts encountered in oral and maxillofacial surgery, account for approximately 33% of all jaw cysts [1]. Although their exact pathogenesis remains incompletely understood, cyst formation is generally attributed to abnormal proliferation of the reduced enamel epithelium [1]. When tooth eruption is impeded, fluid accumulation occurs between the reduced enamel epithelium and the dental crown. This process is mediated by inflammatory cytokines (e.g., interleukin-6 [IL-6], tumor necrosis factor-alpha [TNF-α]), which promote epithelial cell proliferation within the cyst wall and activate matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), ultimately leading to the development of a pathological cavity [2]. The cyst wall typically attaches to the cementoenamel junction and envelops the entire dental crown [3]. Histogenetically, dentigerous cysts are classified into two subtypes: (1) developmental dentigerous cysts, often associated with ectopic tooth positioning or insufficient eruption space, and (2) inflammatory dentigerous cysts, which usually arise secondary to chronic periapical infections in deciduous teeth [4]. Epidemiological studies indicate an incidence of 1.44 dentigerous cysts per 100 unerupted teeth, representing the second most prevalent (14–24%) odontogenic cyst subtype [5]. The condition demonstrates a male predominance (male-to-female ratio 1.5:1) with peak occurrence between 20 and 40 years of age, and 87% of cases involve the mandibular third molar region, while only 3.2% affect mandibular first molars [5].

The management of odontogenic cysts typically involves surgical enucleation of the cystic lining combined with extraction of the associated impacted tooth [6]. During these procedures, particularly in the mandibular molar region, inferior alveolar nerve (IAN) injury represents a common complication occurring in approximately 1.2% of cases [7]. Such injuries may lead to persistent lower lip paresthesia or dysesthesia, significantly compromising patients’ quality of life [7]. A prospective cohort study identified the following risk factors for IAN injury: (1) direct root-mandibular canal contact; (2) apical root position inferior to the inferior alveolar canal; (3) use of chisels during surgery; (4) postoperative omission of corticosteroid therapy; and (5) compressive contact between tooth roots and the inferior alveolar canal [8]. Therefore, preoperative three-dimensional assessment of root-neural relationships and meticulous minimally invasive surgical techniques are critical for preventing neurological complications.

This report presents a rare case of dentigerous cyst associated with an impacted low-positioned mandibular first molar exhibiting complete root encirclement of the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle. Radiographic evaluation revealed the inferior alveolar canal traversing through the root bifurcation zone. Through minimally invasive extraction utilizing piezoelectric osteotomy and controlled root sectioning, complete neurosensory recovery was achieved within one week postoperatively, and no cyst recurrence was observed during one-year follow-up. The uniqueness of this case lies in the coexistence of two high-risk anatomical features: (1) low-positioned impaction of mandibular first molar with cystic involvement, and (2) a complete 180° nerve-encircling configuration formed by bifid roots. This article provides a comprehensive description of the surgical protocol, evidence-based postoperative care strategies, and long-term monitoring approaches for such complex presentations.

Case presentation

Medical history

A 35-year-old woman presented to Liaocheng people’s hospital with a chief complaint of persistent left posterior mandibular buccal swelling lasting over one year.

Specialist examination

The examination revealed maxillofacial asymmetry and pronounced swelling in the left cheek. The patient exhibited a normal range and pattern of mouth opening. Tooth #36 was absent intraorally, and buccal bone swelling was noted in the region of tooth #35-#37. There was no discernible “ping-pong” sensation or tenderness, and the mucosal surface color appeared normal.

Imaging examination

Panoramic radiography demonstrated a deeply impacted tooth #36 in mesioangular position between teeth #35 and #37, associated with a unilocular radiolucent lesion. The lesion exhibited a well-corticated periphery surrounding the dental crown, with characteristic apical extension terminating at the cementoenamel junction (Fig. 1). CBCT revealed a low-positioned impaction with bifid roots demonstrating complete circumferential encasement of the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle (Fig. 2).

Preoperative diagnosis

Low-positioned impaction of mandibular left first molar (#36) with associated dentigerous cyst.

Treatment plan

Perform comprehensive preoperative evaluation including medical comorbidity assessment and surgical risk stratification.2).Following detailed patient education and informed consent acquisition, implement surgical enucleation of the odontogenic cyst with concurrent extraction of the impacted tooth (#36).

Treatment process

The oral mucosa was disinfected with 0.2% chlorhexidine solution. A standard inferior alveolar nerve block anesthesia was administered using 2% lidocaine. After the anesthesia took effect, a #15 scalpel blade was used to create a vertical incision at the mesio-buccal gingiva of tooth #35, followed by a horizontal incision extending distally along the gingival margin to the region of tooth #38. A periosteal elevator was utilized to reflect the mucoperiosteal flap, exposing the bone surface. A piezoelectric ultrasonic bone scalpel was used to remove a portion of the bone at the site of maximal bulging, revealing the cystic wall. After complete enucleation of the cyst, the impacted tooth #36 was exposed. The ultrasonic bone scalpel was then employed along the developmental groove to section the crown and roots of tooth #36, dividing the mesial and distal roots into buccal and lingual segments for separate removal. Throughout the procedure, care was taken to minimize forces applied toward the direction of the neurovascular canal. Upon extraction, the inferior alveolar nerve was visualized within the bony cavity (Fig. 3). The surgical site was irrigated with normal saline and closed with 3 − 0 Vicryl sutures. Standard postoperative instructions and precautions for tooth extraction were provided to the patient.

Postoperative management

Oral medications included Amoxicillin (500 mg three times daily), Methylprednisolone (4 mg once daily), and Mecobalamin (0.5 mg three times daily). Intermittent cold compression was applied to the surgical area within the first 24 h. Patients were instructed to avoid tooth brushing and oral rinsing for 24 h, maintain a bland liquid diet, and return for suture removal 7 days postoperatively.

Postoperative follow-up

At 2 h post-surgery, resolution of anesthesia revealed transient lower lip paresthesia with mild discomfort. Sutures were removed at the 1-week follow-up with confirmation of primary intention healing. By this interval, resolution of lip paresthesia was documented. Panoramic radiography at 6 months demonstrated complete osseous regeneration without evidence of recurrence (Fig. 4). The 12-month evaluation confirmed asymptomatic recovery with no cortical expansion or abnormal tooth mobility in the operative site.

Discussion

Anatomical peculiarities

Distribution characteristics of dentigerous cysts

Dentigerous cysts exhibit marked anatomical predilection, with approximately 80% of cases associated with impacted mandibular third molars [1, 9]. This prevalence may stem from abnormal follicular epithelium proliferation due to prolonged tooth retention. In contrast, the present case occurred at the mandibular left first molar region - an exceptionally rare anatomical location. Sargod et al. reported only one comparable case, suggesting potential pathological mechanisms involving inflammatory stimulation of dental follicle cells during first molar eruption [5]. This rarity necessitates clinical vigilance for dentigerous cysts in non-third-molar impactions, particularly when imaging reveals follicular space > 3 mm [10].

Complexity of root-neural relationships

While the average apex-to-canal distance for mandibular first molars measures 4.92 ± 0.41 mm [11] and direct root-canal contact occurs in merely 3% of cases [12, 13], this case demonstrated complete bifid root encirclement of the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle on CBCT (Fig. 1A). Such configurations represent < 1% of occurrences [14], frequently coinciding with neurovascular variations (e.g., bifurcation or curvature). These cases mandate: (1) preoperative CBCT evaluation of three-dimensional root-neural topography [15], and (2) strategic tooth sectioning (crown-root separation) to prevent neural traction injury during extraction.

Evidence-based treatment strategies

The management of dentigerous cysts requires size-dependent therapeutic decisions. Primary interventions include cyst enucleation with extraction of the involved tooth and marsupialization [16]. Both modalities demonstrate favorable prognoses but carry distinct limitations: Marsupialization for third molar-associated cysts often necessitates extended treatment durations and secondary curettage, while enucleation with extraction typically results in tooth loss and significant bone defects [16].

For teeth with functional potential, orthodontic extrusion may be considered [17]. One documented case involved a 10-year-old male with low-positioned impaction of the mandibular left first molar and associated dentigerous cyst, where 42-month orthodontic treatment successfully restored dental arch integrity and occlusal function [18]. In our case, inadequate eruption space precluded natural tooth emergence, and the patient elected extraction following cyst curettage due to the significant treatment duration required for orthodontic intervention.

Piezoelectric ultrasonic bone scalpel was utilized for osteotomy and tooth sectioning during segmental extraction. This technology preserves osteoblasts and osteocytes, accelerating osseous healing while reducing postoperative complications in complex procedures (e.g., sinus augmentation, ridge splitting) [19]. It demonstrates enhanced patient safety and reduced impact on quality-of-life measures [20].

For post-enucleation osseous defects, autogenous bone grafting (ABG) and guided bone regeneration (GBR) represent primary reconstruction approaches [21]. ABG remains the gold standard for alveolar reconstruction, particularly in complex defects. However, its requirement for secondary surgical sites carries high resorption rates and significant donor-site morbidity [22]. Recent evidence supports autogenous tooth-derived graft materials for cyst-related defects [23], though meta-analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in spontaneous bone healing versus grafting after cyst removal [24]. Through shared decision-making, elective avoidance of graft materials was implemented for this patient.

Teeth demonstrating radicular involvement by cysts can typically be preserved through root canal therapy (RCT) to maintain occlusal function. However, when mobility > 2 mm or periodontal attachment loss > 5 mm is present, the prognosis becomes unfavorable and extraction is indicated. Hemisection has been documented as a viable alternative for preserving adjacent teeth with cyst involvement [25]. In this case, neighboring teeth exhibited no significant cyst involvement, and postoperative pulp vitality testing confirmed normal responses, warranting only watchful monitoring.

Although dentigerous cysts represent common odontogenic lesions, they carry malignant transformation potential—either developing into benign tumors like ameloblastoma [26]or undergoing carcinomatous change [27].Consequently, comprehensive histopathological examination remains mandatory even when clinical presentation suggests benignancy. In our patient, postoperative histopathology confirmed a conventional dentigerous cyst lining without dysplastic features.

Scientific rationale for postoperative management

According to a Cochrane systematic review [28], post-extraction antibiotics reduce infection and alveolar osteitis risks. Short-course corticosteroids effectively minimize postoperative edema and pain without significantly increasing infection rates [29]. Mecobalamin promotes recovery from IAN injuries [30]. Given this patient’s deep impaction with circumferential nerve-root relationships—stratified as high-risk for IAN injury—we implemented an evidence-based pharmacological protocol: antibiotic prophylaxis complemented by oral corticosteroids and mecobalamin supplementation.

Limitations

The neurosensory assessment protocol lacked quantitative objective measures (e.g., Semmes-Weinstein monofilament testing), relying solely on subjective patient reports. Insufficient long-term follow-up necessitates extension to a minimum 5-year surveillance period to evaluate recurrence and neural recovery trajectories.

Conclusion

The impaction of mandibular first molars with concurrent odontogenic cysts represents a clinically rare entity, particularly when complicated by complete root encirclement of the inferior alveolar neurovascular bundle. Following cyst enucleation, clinicians should consider orthodontic traction for functionally viable teeth or select surgical extraction when preservation is contraindicated. Preoperative CBCT evaluation remains mandatory to delineate three-dimensional root-neural topography. Staged surgical intervention—incorporating precision osteotomy, cyst decompression, and neurovascular liberation—effectively mitigates risks of neural injury and osseous defects.
